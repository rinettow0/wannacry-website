01 / INTRODUCTION

WHAT WAS WANNACRY?

WannaCry was a self-spreading ransomware worm that erupted on 12 May 2017. Within a single day it encrypted files on an estimated 200,000+ computers across more than 150 countries, demanding payment in Bitcoin to restore access.

Ransomware is malicious software that locks a victim's files using strong encryption. The attacker then displays a ransom note demanding money in exchange for the decryption key. What made WannaCry exceptional was that it did not rely on a user clicking a bad link — it spread automatically from machine to machine across networks.

The incident became globally significant because it struck hospitals, factories, railways and telecom providers at once, proving how a single unpatched vulnerability can cascade into a worldwide crisis.

ransom_note.txt
!!! Ooops, your files have been encrypted !!!
> Send $300 worth of Bitcoin to this address.
> Payment doubles after 3 days.
> Files deleted after 7 days.
# — WannaCry ransom screen, May 2017

Encryption

Used AES + RSA to lock documents, photos and databases, appending the .WNCRY extension.

Worm Behaviour

Scanned local and internet networks for vulnerable machines and infected them without any human action.

Ransom

Demanded $300–$600 in Bitcoin, with a countdown timer pressuring victims to pay quickly.

02 / BACKGROUND

THE STORY BEHIND THE ATTACK

WannaCry was powered by a leaked cyber-weapon. Understanding its origins reveals why patching matters.

Windows SMBv1 Flaw

A vulnerability in the old Server Message Block (SMBv1) file-sharing protocol let code execute remotely on Windows systems.

EternalBlue

An exploit originally developed by the U.S. NSA that weaponised the SMBv1 flaw to break into machines silently.

The Shadow Brokers

A hacking group that stole and publicly leaked NSA exploits, including EternalBlue, in April 2017.

MS17-010 Patch

Microsoft had already released a fix on 14 March 2017 — but countless systems remained unpatched.

INTERACTIVE TIMELINE

14 MAR 2017

A critical security update patches the SMBv1 vulnerability, two months before the outbreak.

14 APR 2017

WannaCry spreads worldwide in hours. The UK's NHS is among the first major victims to go public.

Organisations rush to patch systems. Investigations and attribution efforts begin worldwide.

03 / ATTACK ANALYSIS

HOW THE ATTACK WORKED

A defensive, educational breakdown of the six stages — how the worm moved and why it was so destructive.

01

Vulnerability Exploitation

The worm located machines running unpatched SMBv1 and used the EternalBlue exploit to gain entry without credentials.

02

Network Propagation

Once inside, it scanned local and internet-facing networks, copying itself to every reachable vulnerable host.

03

Ransomware Installation

The payload installed itself as a service so it could persist and run with high privileges on the system.

04

File Encryption

Documents, images and databases were encrypted with strong cryptography and renamed with a .WNCRY extension.

05

Ransom Demand

A lock screen demanded $300–$600 in Bitcoin, with a countdown threatening permanent data loss.

06

Further Infection

Each infected machine became a new launch point, allowing the worm to keep spreading automatically.

06 / PREVENTION

HOW COULD THIS HAVE BEEN PREVENTED?

A professional protection checklist. Most WannaCry infections were preventable with basic cyber-hygiene.

OS Updates

Keep operating systems current so known flaws are closed quickly.

Security Patches

Apply critical patches like MS17-010 without delay.

Regular Backups

Keep offline, tested backups so you can recover without paying.

Endpoint Protection

Use reputable antivirus/EDR to detect and block malware.

Network Segmentation

Divide networks so one infection cannot reach everything.

Disable SMBv1

Turn off the obsolete SMBv1 protocol that WannaCry abused.

Staff Awareness

Train employees to spot phishing and report incidents fast.

Firewall Config

Block risky ports (e.g. 445) at the network boundary.

08 / LESSONS LEARNED

WHAT DID WANNACRY TEACH THE WORLD?

01

Updates Are Critical

A patch existed two months before the attack. Timely updates would have stopped most infections.

02

Backups Are Essential

Organisations with clean, offline backups could restore data instead of paying a ransom.

03

Strategy Beats Chaos

Clear incident-response plans let teams react fast instead of improvising under pressure.

04

People Matter

Aware employees are a strong line of defence against phishing and social engineering.

05

Prevention Over Recovery

Preventing an incident is far cheaper and safer than cleaning one up afterwards.

09 / CONCLUSION

ONE UNPATCHED SYSTEM CAN PUT AN ENTIRE NETWORK AT RISK.

WannaCry was a wake-up call for the digital world. A single leaked exploit, combined with unpatched machines, caused a global crisis within hours — paralysing hospitals, factories and public services. Its true lesson is timeless: cybersecurity is a shared responsibility, and basic discipline — patching, backups, and awareness — is the strongest shield against even the most advanced threats.
